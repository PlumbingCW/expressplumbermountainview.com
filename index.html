When a plumbing problem is an emergency

Plenty of plumbing trouble can wait until morning. A slow drain, a dripping faucet and a toilet that runs after flushing are annoying but stable. An emergency is anything that is damaging the structure, threatening the electrical system or leaving the household without a safe water or sewer connection.

In Mountain View the calls that usually cannot wait are a burst or split supply line, sewage coming up through a floor drain or tub, and a water heater leaking from the tank body. A supply valve that will not shut off and a meter that keeps spinning with every fixture closed belong on the list too. Each of those turns into drywall, flooring and mold cost faster than most owners expect.

If you are unsure whether it counts, call. We would rather talk you through it and tell you it can wait than have you mop for three hours first.

What happens when you call the emergency line

  1. A person answers. You describe what you see and hear, and we ask where the main shutoff is and whether anything is still running.
  2. Dispatch is arranged. We send the closest available licensed plumber and give you a clear price before work starts. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.
  3. The plumber stops the damage first. Shutting off, draining down or clearing the line comes before any permanent repair.
  4. You decide on the permanent fix. You hear the options and the cost, then choose. Nothing proceeds without that conversation.

Mountain View homes and the plumbing under them

Mountain View is not one housing era. Monta Loma, bounded by San Antonio Road, Middlefield Road, Rengstorff Avenue and Central Expressway, grew from three developments, Eichler's Fairview, Mackay's Oakwood and Mardell Manor. The City's historic context statement dates the Fairview Eichlers to roughly 1954 to 1957.

Many mid-century homes like these sit on slabs, and Eichler homes often hide radiant heating pipe in the floor, so a warm patch or a climbing water bill points toward slab leak repair before anything else.

Blossom Valley is a set of 1950s and 1960s ranch-style subdivisions built on former orchard land, with the Gest Ranch and Miramonte Oaks pocket along the Los Altos side. Homes that age usually have decades-old supply piping and clay or cast iron sewer laterals, which is why root-clogged drains and low pressure are common service calls there. A drain that keeps returning is often a sewer camera inspection question, not a clog question.

Cuernavaca, off Crestview Drive near the Sunnyvale line, sits on a former cherry orchard, and downtown around Castro Street mixes older single-family streets with newer apartments and mixed-use buildings. Newer buildings change the call: shared risers, tight utility closets and tankless water heaters show up more than galvanized pipe.

Water, sewer and permits in Mountain View

Public water reports that describe the City's supply put about 89 percent of it from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, with Valley Water and a small share of local groundwater making up the rest. The City's water quality page also states that its 2024 inventory found no lead service lines. That says nothing about the fixtures and solder inside an older house, which is a separate question.

Wastewater leaves the city through a shared system. The Palo Alto Regional Water Quality Control Plant treats sewage for Mountain View, Los Altos, Los Altos Hills, Palo Alto, Stanford and the East Palo Alto Sanitary District. If a backup is in the street main rather than your lateral, the City's Public Works wastewater section is the right call alongside ours.

The Building Division at 500 Castro Street says a permit is required for every water heater installation and replacement, and that it must be obtained before work starts. The same guide asks for two seismic straps, one in the top third of the tank and one in the bottom third. We build that into water heater replacement jobs rather than treating it as an extra.

Emergency plumber questions in Mountain View

Do you handle plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Mountain View?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call (650) 860-1670, describe what is happening, and a licensed plumber is dispatched to your address.

How fast can a plumber reach me?

It depends on traffic on El Camino Real, US-101 and Central Expressway and on who is closest when you call. We tell you the dispatch window on the phone and do not promise an exact arrival time we cannot control.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Shut off the main water valve, turn off the water heater if it is leaking, keep everyone away from wet outlets and appliances, and move valuables out of the wet area. For a sewer backup, stop running water in the house. Photograph the damage for your insurer before cleanup.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. The plumber diagnoses first and gives a clear price before work starts. If the scope changes mid-job, you hear about it before more work is done.

Do you replace water heaters, and do you pull the permit?

We replace tank and tankless water heaters. The City of Mountain View requires a permit for every water heater installation and replacement, and the work includes seismic strapping. We handle that as part of the job.

Do you serve Los Altos, Sunnyvale and Palo Alto too?

Yes. Los Altos has its own page because its water provider, sewer rules and housing mix differ from Mountain View. Sunnyvale, Palo Alto and Cupertino are served from the same dispatch line.

Are you licensed and insured?

The plumbers we dispatch are licensed and insured. California residents can check any contractor's license on the Contractors State License Board website before work begins, and you should.
