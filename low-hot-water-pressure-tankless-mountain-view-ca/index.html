Low Hot Water Pressure on a Tankless Heater: Mountain View

By the Mountain View Express Plumbing team. Published October 16, 2026. Updated October 16, 2026.

Weak hot water from a tankless heater is a common complaint. The key clue is whether cold water at the same tap is strong. If cold is strong and hot is weak, the problem is in the hot side. If both are weak, it is a household pressure or supply problem.

This guide helps you narrow it down before you call.

Start with one test

Open a hot tap fully, then a cold tap at the same fixture. Compare them. Check other fixtures too. A problem at one fixture is a local problem. A problem everywhere on hot only points to the heater or the hot lines.

What the pattern suggests
PatternLikely causeNext step
Hot weak everywhere, cold strongHeater scale, clogged inlet screen or partly closed valveCheck valves, flush the unit
Both weak everywhereSupply pressure or partly closed mainCheck the main valve and pressure
Weak at one fixtureAerator or cartridgeClean or replace

Common causes on the heater

Scale in the heat exchanger

Minerals build up inside the exchanger and restrict flow. A descaling flush restores it. The manual gives the interval, and heavy scale often shows up as error codes too.

A clogged inlet screen

Many units have a small screen where water enters. Sediment can clog it. It can usually be removed and rinsed.

Isolation valves not fully open

After service, valves left half open choke flow. Check that the handles line up with the pipe.

A flow limit built into the unit

Some units reduce flow to reach the set temperature at high demand. Lowering the setpoint raises flow, which is why a lower temperature can feel stronger.

Causes outside the heater

  • Corroded or scaled hot water pipes, particularly in older homes.
  • A pressure regulator that is failing or set low.
  • A partially closed main or branch valve.
  • A leak somewhere in the hot line, which the leak detection page covers.

If hot pipes are corroded, no heater service will cure the flow. That is a repiping discussion.

What a plumber checks

  1. Measure pressure. A gauge on a hose bib shows static pressure into the house.
  2. Check flow at the heater. Gallons per minute through the unit show whether the unit or the pipes are the limit.
  3. Inspect screens and valves. Sediment and half-open valves are common.
  4. Flush and test. A flush restores the exchanger, and a retest confirms the fix.

Tankless water heater service explains flushing and repair in more detail.

Flushing a tankless unit step by step

  1. Power down. Turn off the unit and close the gas and water supply.
  2. Connect hoses. Attach hoses to the service ports on the isolation valves, one to a pump and bucket, one back into the bucket.
  3. Circulate descaler. Run a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for the time the manual states.
  4. Rinse. Flush with clean water until it runs clear.
  5. Restore and test. Reopen the valves, power up and check flow and temperature.

A flush needs isolation valves with service ports. If your unit lacks them, a plumber can add them so the job is simple next time.

Checking the inlet screen

Most units have a small filter screen where cold water enters. It catches sediment before it reaches the heat exchanger. Turn off the water, unscrew the inlet fitting and slide the screen out. Rinse it under a tap. A screen packed with grit can cut flow noticeably, and cleaning it costs nothing.

If you find a lot of debris, the supply line or the main may be shedding sediment. A whole-house sediment filter is worth discussing with a plumber.

When pipes, not the heater, are the problem

Old galvanized or corroded copper pipes narrow from the inside. Hot lines can be affected more than cold because heat speeds corrosion and scale. If the heater is clean and flow is still weak at several fixtures, the pipes are the likely cause. That is a diagnosis for a plumber with a flow gauge.

Setting the temperature to help flow

A tankless unit raises water temperature by a fixed amount at a given flow. The higher the setpoint, the less flow it can heat. If your unit is set to a very high temperature, it may cut flow at a busy tap to hit the target. Lowering the setpoint a little can raise flow without a big change in comfort.

Check the water temperature at your tap with a thermometer. Many households set the heater higher than they need and then mix with cold at the faucet, which wastes capacity. A lower setpoint also reduces scald risk for children and older adults.

Safety notes while you troubleshoot

Turn off gas and power before opening any panel or disconnecting a fitting. Do not remove a vent or alter the gas connection yourself. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Tankless units run hot enough to scald. When you test flow at the tap, use a thermometer and keep children away from the tap. If you are not comfortable with a step, stop and call a plumber.

Mountain View supply pressure

The City of Mountain View's water FAQ says the system delivers an average minimum pressure of 45 pounds, above the state requirement of 25 pounds. That is the pressure on the City side. What reaches your tap depends on your plumbing, the pressure regulator if you have one and the condition of your pipes.

If both hot and cold are weak, ask a plumber to measure pressure at a hose bib. The same applies in Los Altos, where your water provider's pressure may differ.

Questions about this topic

Why is the hot water weak but the cold strong?

Usually the heater is restricting flow, from scale, a clogged inlet screen or valves not fully open.

Will flushing fix low hot water pressure?

Often, when scale is the cause. It will not help if pipes are corroded.

Does a tankless unit lower flow on purpose?

It can at high demand to reach the set temperature. Lowering the setpoint may raise flow.

What pressure does Mountain View deliver?

The City says average minimum pressure is 45 pounds. Pressure at your tap depends on your home's plumbing.

Is low pressure a sign I need new pipes?

Possibly, if it affects several fixtures and the pipe is old. A plumber can measure and advise.
